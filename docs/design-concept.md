# Design Concept

> Note: this replaces the earlier "hidden frogs on a grid" concept. The core
> find-the-hidden-thing loop carries over; theme, characters, and content model
> are new.

## Title

**Dead Lines Inc.**

## Premise

A cute, ironic cartoon afterlife. The player joins a corporation whose job is
to usher departed souls to the other side. The corporation is run by
**Death** — cute but strict, classic black robe, scythe, a skull with glowing
green eye-lights.

## Core Loop

1. Lost souls hide on the game field.
2. The player finds them.
3. A found soul is "released" and joins a queue leading to the corporation's
   gates, on its way to the afterlife.

## Tone

- Comedic, not scary.
- Humor readable by both adults and kids.
- Satire target: corporate bureaucracy and office culture, applied to the
  afterlife — Beetlejuice-flavored, with the wit of Terry Pratchett's *Mort*
  (Death runs a shop; the shop has rules, forms, and quotas).

## Souls (collectible content)

- ~500 distinct soul types.
- Found souls are collected into an achievement/collection list.
- Each soul has a distinguishing trait: mustache, glasses, wig, saber, a
  paint palette, etc.
- Some souls are recognizable riffs on historical figures (Napoleon, Dalí,
  and others).

## Characters

### Death

- Corporation's head/boss.
- Cute but strict — authority figure, not a threat.
- Visual: canonical black robe, scythe, skull with green glowing eyes.

## Open Questions

- Visual/art style direction (flat vector vs. painterly cartoon, palette).
- How souls are hidden/revealed on the field (mechanic detail, difficulty
  curve, field size).
- How the ~500 soul roster is organized (categories, rarity tiers, unlock
  pacing).
- Additional corporation cast beyond Death (co-workers, HR, etc.) — implied
  by "office culture" satire but not yet specified.
